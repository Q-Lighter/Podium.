The framework behind your feedback
Every piece of feedback you get is grounded in one of these principles — not just opinion.

Filler words

Words like "um," "uh," "like," and "so" are natural in conversation, but in short bursts they signal hesitation to an audience. Reducing filler density — not eliminating it entirely — is what moves the needle on how confident you sound.

Pacing (words per minute)

Most confident, comprehensible speech lands around 120–160 words per minute. Faster often reads as nervous energy; much slower can lose an audience's attention.

Structure

A speech with a clear opening hook, a logical throughline, and a real conclusion is dramatically more memorable than one that rambles or trails off.

Concrete examples over abstraction

Specific stories, numbers, and examples are more persuasive and memorable than abstract claims.

Confidence markers vs. hedging

Phrases like "I think maybe," "sort of," and "I guess" quietly undercut your authority. Swapping hedged language for direct statements measurably changes perceived competence.

Audience connection

Rhetorical questions, direct address, and relatable framing pull a passive listener into active engagement.

Handling interjections (pitch mode)

In real pitches and interviews, being interrupted is normal — investors and interviewers probe live. The skill being trained is composure under interruption: answering the actual question asked, briefly, then steering back to your core point rather than losing your thread or restarting from scratch.
